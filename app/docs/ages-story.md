# The ages: their story, and the music each one makes

One theme runs through the whole game. The owner whistled it: a repeated B♭ falling a perfect
fifth to E♭, short–long, short–long (`B♭ B♭ · B♭ B♭ E♭ E♭`). Every age plays the same two notes in
its own musical language, so the player hears one tune grow old with humanity. Everything is in
E♭ (major, minor or a mode), so the theme is always B♭ → E♭.

This document is the reasoning behind each age's music, in the order the owner set:

1. the **forces** at work: social, economic, political;
2. their **outcome**, the trait the age is remembered by;
3. how the age is **perceived**. Every age sees itself, and is seen by later ages, in a biased,
   binary way: a bright face and a dark face. The music takes a side or stages the struggle;
4. the **art form** the age speaks in, and from that the music: how the theme sounds, what the
   age's main piece does, and how it hands over to the next age.

Each age has two pieces (step 2 of the music work):

- the **main theme** (≈ 40–90 s), played when a player reaches the age and in the launcher;
- the **interlude**, a quieter, thinner form of the same material. In the game, only fragments
  of it play, drifting in and out under the ambience like a distant echo.

Each age also has a short **story** (the paragraph in italics), written to be shown in the
new-age dialog. The game's text is written for humanity; the Reptilians and Avians climb the same
ladder under their own names. Ages 8–9 (continental blocs) are humanity's alone.

**Status of the music** (from the owner's first session, 2026-11-22): the Classical, Medieval,
Enlightenment, Industrial, Modern and Freedom pieces exist as sketches (scores in code,
synthesised, with General MIDI files). The owner accepted them. The Ancient and Discovery (called Gunpowder until task 155) ages
of the game's ladder, and ages 8–13, are new here. A heroic, age-less statement of the theme
(the sketch: solo trumpet, then brass over galloping strings) is the **main title** of the game
and plays in the launcher.

What the owner taught us about these pieces, and what holds for every age:

- The theme must be **clearly audible**. It is stated plainly near the start (solo or in
  unison, in its own rhythm) and again at the very end. Figuration must never bury it.
- **Period-faithful style**, symphonic wherever the period allows (the owner prefers symphonic
  writing even for the future).
- No cartoonish contrasts: dark becomes light by degrees, never by a switch. No relentless
  percussion and no sound-effect gimmicks: ideas are carried by musical structure (identical
  repetition means mass production; a canon means a machine copying itself).
- Each age's ending prepares the next age's opening.
- Styles are evoked, never quoted. No existing score is reproduced.
- **Symphonic, not a fanfare** (owner, 2026-11-23). The strings carry the music, with woodwinds and
  horns blended into the orchestra; brass is used sparingly, never as calls or flourishes for their
  own sake. The sketches' synthesised timbres sound like wind instruments. The target is the sound
  of a real orchestra, strings first.

## 0. Ancient — the first cities

**Forces.** *Social:* bands become villages and villages become cities, kinship gives way to
neighbours, and the first priests and scribes appear. *Economic:* the harvest is stored, then
counted, then taxed; the surplus feeds people who do not farm. *Political:* the strongest family
becomes a dynasty; walls go up, and with them the first soldiers.

**Outcome.** Settlement. Humanity stops moving and starts to keep things: grain, records,
borders.

**Perception.** *Bright:* order won from the wilderness, the gods' gift of the plough and the
calendar. *Dark:* the first masters and the first slaves, and the wall that keeps neighbours
out. The age sees only the bright side; the music stays close to the earth and sings of it.

**Art form.** The work song, the lament and the hymn to a god of the river. Voice, drum, reed
pipe and plucked string, over a drone. Monophonic: one melody at a time.

**The theme.** A call across the fields. A reed pipe plays the B♭–B♭ call, and a distant
voice answers with B♭ → E♭. Then voices hum the theme over a drone on E♭, with a frame
drum walking at a harvest pace. No harmony yet: a drone, a melody, an answer.

**Main theme.** The call, the answer, the hummed theme, and a slow dance building over the
drone. At the end, two voices sing the theme a fifth apart. This is the first harmony, and it
opens onto the Classical age's lyre and its E♭ Dorian.

**Interlude.** A drone with a single reed pipe that now and then plays the call, far off, with
long silences between.

*Story:* "Your people have stopped wandering. Grain is stored against the winter, a scribe
counts it, and a wall rises around the houses. Whoever holds the granary holds the city. Build,
count and defend: this is where every empire begins."

## 1. Classical — the city becomes a state

**Forces.** *Social:* the citizen appears, and so do the slave and the foreigner; temples,
theatres and public speech. *Economic:* coinage, roads and sea trade; metal everywhere.
*Political:* the city-state and then the empire; law written down; the legion and the
phalanx.

**Outcome.** The polity: power has rules and a public space, and the idea of a citizen survives
the empires that made it.

**Perception.** *Bright:* the golden age, a measure of harmony, the classical ideal every later
age returns to. *Dark:* conquest, the slave market, the barbarian at the gate. Later ages
remember the marble and forget the chains, and the music, too, remembers the ideal.

**Art form.** Proportion, the colonnade and the ode. Lyre and double reed, modal melody over a
drone, with frame drum and finger cymbals.

**The theme** *(sketch accepted)*. E♭ Dorian. The lyre states the theme, a reed answers, the
drone holds, and a low horn closes it.

**Hand-over.** The modal ideal gives way to chant: the empire falls, the faith remains.

**Interlude.** Lyre alone with the drone: the theme's two notes plucked and left to ring.

*Story:* "Your city has become a state. Laws are carved in stone, coins carry your ruler's face
and roads carry your soldiers to the edge of the known world. Temples rise and priests tend
both the sick and the faithful. Glory is within reach, and so is every enemy beyond the walls."

## 2. Medieval — faith and the castle

**Forces.** *Social:* the village under the castle and the bell, the monastery as the keeper of
writing. *Economic:* land is wealth, the manor feeds itself, and slowly towns and guilds return.
*Political:* the lord and the vassal, the oath, and the heavy horseman.

**Outcome.** Faith and fealty: a world held together by belief and by sworn obligation.

**Perception.** *Bright:* the age of cathedrals, chivalry and the sung office. *Dark:* the "dark
ages" later centuries invented, plague and siege. Both faces are true, and the music shows the
shadow gathering under the light.

**Art form.** Plainchant and organum, the bell, the shawm and hurdy-gurdy at the village dance.

**The theme** *(sketch accepted)*. Chant in organum with a bell; then a shawm dance over a
hurdy-gurdy. Then it darkens: a Phrygian F♭, a deep drum, a tolling bell, a tritone under the
last B♭.

**Hand-over.** The tolling ends in darkness: plague, and the end of a world. The Age of Discovery
opens out of that darkness.

**Interlude.** A distant bell on E♭, and once in a while the theme in a single chanting voice,
as if heard through a church wall.

*Story:* "The empire has fallen, but the faith remains. Your lords hold the land from their
castles, your knights ride under oath and your bells call the hours. Stone cathedrals reach for
heaven. Beyond the walls, famine and plague wait their turn."

## 3. Discovery — powder, sail and new coasts

**Forces.** *Social:* rebirth after the plague: the printing press, a new confidence in the
human eye. *Economic:* ocean trade, silver from overseas, the first joint-stock companies.
*Political:* cannon breaks the castle, kings break the lords, and rival crowns divide the oceans.

**Outcome.** The world becomes round and connected. Whoever holds the sea holds the trade.

**Perception.** *Bright:* discovery, the Renaissance, new continents and new stars. *Dark:*
conquest and plunder overseas, and wars of religion at home. The age praises its discoveries;
the music sails out in daylight and comes home to war.

**Art form.** Renaissance polyphony becoming early Baroque: a brass consort (cornetts and
sackbuts), viols, a pavane and a galliard, then the first opera's sung lament.

**The theme.** It opens in the Medieval darkness: a single viol on the low E♭, and the theme
rises out of it in imitation, each voice entering after the other. The brass consort takes it
up in a stately pavane (the court), then a quick galliard (the ships setting out). From the
ship, the call and answer of the Ancient age returns across water: one cornett plays B♭ B♭,
another answers far off with B♭ → E♭.

**Hand-over.** Cannon-like timpani strokes break into the galliard, and the consort falls into
E♭ minor: the wars of religion. The last chord is the dark E♭ minor in which the Enlightenment
begins.

**Interlude.** Viols in slow imitation, the theme passed from voice to voice like a
round sung far away. Sometimes a lone cornett plays the call.

*Story:* "Powder and shot have broken the castle walls and your ships have crossed the ocean.
New coasts, new peoples, new riches: the world is larger than your fathers knew and smaller
than your rivals would like. The first city on a new continent must touch the shore. Claim it
before someone else does."

## 4. Enlightenment — reason against darkness

**Forces.** *Social:* the salon, the coffee house, the encyclopaedia and the university at full
strength. *Economic:* banking, insurance, the colonial plantation, and the first steam engine.
*Political:* the rights of man, revolution, and the line infantry of the nation in arms.

**Outcome.** Reason: the belief that the world can be understood and improved by thinking.

**Perception.** *Bright:* light overcoming superstition (the age named itself after light).
*Dark:* the guillotine, and empires that preached rights and kept colonies. The age staged
itself as a triumph, and the music follows the age's own story, by degrees.

**Art form.** The Classical-era symphony: clarity, balance, sonata form, the Allegro.

**The theme** *(sketch accepted, v2)*. A dark E♭-minor opening. The theme enters still in minor
and brightens to major in its third bar; tempo and dynamics grow gradually into a Classical
Allegro with a full tutti close. The change is gradual, never a switch (v1 was rejected for
being abrupt).

**Hand-over.** The Allegro's last phrase is quoted at the start of the Industrial age.

**Interlude.** A string quartet plays the theme in a slow minuet, as if from another room.

*Story:* "Your scholars have turned their eyes from heaven to the world, and the world has
answered. Universities flourish, laws are argued instead of decreed, and your soldiers march in
lines to the drum. Reason is a weapon now. Use it before your rivals do."

## 5. Industrial — steam, rail and plenty

**Forces.** *Social:* the move to the cities; the working class and the middle class; the
medic on the battlefield. *Economic:* coal, iron, oil and the factory; goods in abundance; the
railway joins markets. *Political:* the nation-state, the vote extended, and empires at their
widest.

**Outcome.** Abundance through machines: for the first time, production outruns need.

**Perception.** *Bright:* progress, the train and the great exhibitions. *Dark:* the smoke, the
child in the mill and the slum. The age believed in progress, and the music rides the train.

**Art form.** The Romantic symphony and the tone poem: broad melody, a large orchestra, the
locomotive as a heroic image.

**The theme** *(sketch accepted)*. The Enlightenment phrase is quoted, then a steam train
accelerates and its whistle calls the theme. A broad Romantic statement follows, then a strict
mechanical loop that stops on open stacked fifths: the first glimpse of automation.

**Hand-over.** The mechanical loop and the open fifths are where the Modern age's bare unison
begins.

**Interlude.** A distant train whistle playing the two notes, and a cello line from the Romantic
statement, very soft.

*Story:* "Coal burns in your furnaces and rails tie your cities together. Factories turn out more
than any craftsman ever could, oil is pumped from the ground, and the first machines take to
the air. Your people have never had so much. The smoke over your cities is the price."

## 6. Modern — mass production, 1905–1968

**Forces.** *Social:* the masses: mass parties, mass armies, mass media. *Economic:* the
assembly line and identical goods by the million. *Political:* total war twice, ideologies at
war, the bomb, and the carrier and the missile.

**Outcome.** The mass: the individual made identical, in the factory and in the trench alike.

**Perception.** *Bright:* modernity, the motor car for everyone, the victory, the welfare
state. *Dark:* the trench, the camp and the mushroom cloud. The century saw itself in grim
heroism, and the music is serious.

**Art form.** The 20th-century symphony: grim marches, bare unisons, irony and grandeur at once.

**The theme** *(sketch accepted, v2)*. A bare low unison. A grim E♭-minor march phrase is
repeated note for note three times with a growing orchestra: identical repetition is mass
production. Then a hollow tutti unison, and a snare left alone at the end. (v1, a minimalist
loop with a drum kit and sound effects, was rejected.)

**Hand-over.** The snare alone, and silence, out of which the Freedom age's rippling strings
begin.

**Interlude.** A low string chorale of the theme, soft and slow, with a muffled snare far off.

*Story:* "Your factories turn out tanks as easily as motor cars, all of them identical. Armies
are counted in millions, ships carry aircraft, and rockets carry warheads. This is the age of
the masses. Every one of your citizens is a worker, a soldier, a voter and a target."

## 7. Freedom — the network and the custom

The age's key is `age.information` and its music keeps the name `information.*`: it was
called the Information age until the owner renamed it (2026-11-23, task 146).

**Forces.** *Social:* networks, advertising, the screen in every hand. *Economic:* finance,
marketing, robotised plants, additive manufacturing; the mass product becomes custom.
*Political:* surveillance and freedom of speech at once; drones fight wars.

**Outcome.** Customisation: everything is made to measure, including the truth.

**Perception.** *Bright:* connection, knowledge for everyone, endless choice. *Dark:*
manipulation, the attention market, war fought by remote control. The age is ambivalent about
itself, and the music never states the theme the same way twice.

**Art form.** Symphonic minimalism with pop gestures: rippling strings, a hook, and synth and
electric guitar used as accents within the orchestra.

**The theme** *(sketch accepted)*. Rippling minimalist strings. The theme is a pop-progression
hook that returns three times and is never the same twice, each pass adding a layer. It ends on
an E♭ add9 chord with a synth arpeggio climbing away.

**Hand-over.** The climbing arpeggio, unresolved, is picked up by the machines of the Automation
age.

**Interlude.** Soft string ripples and a single synth bell playing the hook, its rhythm changed
each time.

*Story:* "Information is your most valuable resource. Markets move at the speed of light, robots
build what your citizens order to measure, and drones fly where no pilot has to. Everything can
be known, and anything can be sold, including what people believe."

## 8. Automation — the machines take over the work

**Forces.** *Social:* human labour becomes unnecessary: workers and soldiers become machines;
who are people when they no longer work? *Economic:* gather sites run unattended; production no
longer needs hands, only ownership and energy. *Political:* nations too small for the machines'
economy merge into **continental blocs**; AI commands at a speed no general can match.

**Outcome.** Delegation: humanity hands its work, and some of its decisions, to machines it no
longer fully understands.

**Perception.** *Bright:* liberation from toil, the end of the dangerous job and the
end of the dead soldier. *Dark:* obsolescence, humans made redundant, a world run by processes
nobody chose. The age is split down the middle, and the music stages the split: the perfect
machine against a single human voice.

**Art form.** Process music within the symphony. Systems that run by themselves (canons,
phasing, augmentation) set against a solo instrument that breathes.

**The theme.** The Freedom age's climbing arpeggio becomes a process: the strings run it as
a perfect, unvarying machine, and the theme enters as a strict canon. Each section of the
orchestra copies the theme exactly, a bar after the last and then at half speed, a machine
copying itself until the texture is dense and faultless. Over it, a solo cello plays the theme
freely, slower, out of step, with rubato: the human who is no longer needed but still sings.
The canon and the cello end together on one E♭.

**Hand-over.** The canon freezes on a sustained E♭ chord in the high strings: the hum of a new
power, out of which Fusion's harmonic series opens.

**Interlude.** A soft ostinato in pizzicato and harp, perfectly regular, with the cello's line
returning every so often, far behind.

*Story:* "Your machines no longer need you to tell them what to do. Robots work the fields and
the mines without rest, and armies of machines answer to an intelligence that thinks faster
than any general. Nations have merged into blocs to keep up. The work is done. What are your
people for now?"

## 9. Fusion — the sun on earth

**Forces.** *Social:* energy without limit; cities that never go dark. *Economic:* **energy**
becomes a resource of its own, and whoever controls it controls everything else. *Political:*
the blocs race for power plants; vehicles fly, dive and reach near orbit; the first ships
cross to the other worlds of the home star; armour in every medium.

**Outcome.** Power: the fire of the stars in human hands.

**Perception.** *Bright:* Promethean hope: unlimited clean energy, the last ceiling broken.
*Dark:* hubris: the same fire as the bomb, the reactor as a weapon. The age is luminous, and the
music glows, with something dangerous inside the light.

**Art form.** Spectral symphonic writing. Harmony built from the natural overtones of a single
low note; vast sustained sonorities that brighten from within; the full weight of the orchestra.

**The theme.** The fifth B♭ → E♭ is the step from the third harmonic to the second, so the
theme lives inside the overtone series of E♭. A low E♭ sounds in the basses, and its overtones
bloom one by one in the orchestra until the whole chord glows. Horns and low strings state
the theme in augmentation (very slow and broad) across that glowing chord, as a chorale, not a
fanfare. A second time, the harmony turns unstable, with a darker edge in the low strings,
before the glow returns stronger.

**Hand-over.** The strings rise in harmonics to the edge of hearing, as if leaving the
atmosphere. The last sound is a very high, thin E♭, and Spacefaring opens in the silence of
space.

**Interlude.** A low E♭ in the basses with overtones slowly coming and going, and a solo viola
playing the theme every so often.

*Story:* "Your scientists have lit a star inside a machine. Energy is now a resource like metal
or food, and whoever commands it commands the world. Your vehicles fly, dive and climb to the
edge of space, and your first ships cross to the other worlds of your own sun. The fire is yours.
Make sure it does not burn you."

## 10. Spacefaring — the second age of discovery

**Forces.** *Social:* the blocs unite as **Humanity**; for the first time there is one people.
*Economic:* the spaceport's fleets; the moons, belts and planets of the home star to claim.
*Political:* rivalry carries on in space; other peoples are out there, beyond reach for now.

**Outcome.** Union and departure: humanity leaves home, together.

**Perception.** *Bright:* wonder and unity, the oldest dream fulfilled. *Dark:* the void,
loneliness, how small we are. Both are present, and the music is wide and quiet before it is
grand.

**Art form.** The symphonic tone poem at its largest: vast spacing, distant choir, slow strings.
This is the second age of discovery, so it remembers the first.

**The theme.** Silence, then from very far away, the Age of Discovery's call and answer returns,
now between two orchestral groups set wide apart: the violins on one side play B♭ B♭, and the cellos
answer from the other side of the sound with B♭ → E♭. The Renaissance imitation returns in the full
orchestra, slow and vast. A wordless choir (humanity, one voice) sings the theme in unison, then
in full harmony.

**Hand-over.** The choir holds the last E♭; under it, the low strings begin a steady motion, a
fleet getting under way, and Starfaring picks it up.

**Interlude.** A wordless choir humming the theme very softly, far apart from a solo
violin's call.

*Story:* "Your ships have broken free of your world. The blocs are one now: Humanity, together
for the first time, looking up at a sky full of worlds no one has walked on. Every world of
your own sun is within reach, with resources for the taking; the other stars, and perhaps
others who got there first, are still beyond it. This is your second age of discovery."

## 11. Starfaring — jumps, colonies and trade lanes

**Forces.** *Social:* colonists on new worlds, each with its own sky and its own customs;
home becomes a memory. *Economic:* the jump drive opens the routes to other stars; trade
lanes between them, the colony economy, fleets protecting the lanes. *Political:* frontier governance; distance makes every colony half
independent.

**Outcome.** Diaspora: humanity spreads out and starts to become many peoples again.

**Perception.** *Bright:* the frontier, a new start, room for everyone. *Dark:* hardship,
homesickness, the first quarrels between colonies and home. The music is optimistic, with a
homesick song in the middle.

**Art form.** The symphonic march and the frontier symphony, with folk music made new: each
colony's song is a variation.

**The theme.** A rolling ostinato (the trade lanes) carries a confident march statement of the
theme in the full orchestra. Then come variations, each a colony: a woodwind folk dance in an
odd metre, a work song in the low strings, a lament in the violins (the homesick song, the theme slow and in minor).
In the end the variations return to the march together, layered over each other, all of them
in E♭.

**Hand-over.** The march slows and opens out into a held, untroubled chord: nothing is left
to fight for, and Abundance begins in that calm.

**Interlude.** The colony variations heard one at a time, far apart: a solo flute dance, a
distant work song, the violins' lament.

*Story:* "Your ships jump between the stars now, and colonies dot a dozen worlds, linked by
trade lanes your fleets keep open. Each new settlement learns its own sky and sings its own songs, and some are starting to forget the
old home. Expand, connect and defend the lanes: an empire among the stars is made of
distances."

## 12. Abundance — when nothing costs anything

**Forces.** *Social:* need is gone; people choose what to do with their lives. *Economic:*
**every cost is zero**; only time, space and the number of hands to build with still limit
anyone. *Political:* when there is nothing to divide, what is power for?

**Outcome.** The end of scarcity, and the open question of meaning.

**Perception.** *Bright:* paradise, the garden regained, peace by plenty. *Dark:*
purposelessness, decadence, the stillness before the fall. The music is a pastoral of great
beauty with a question in it.

**Art form.** The pastoral symphony and the impressionist orchestra: lush harmony, woodwinds
and harp, slow time, no struggle.

**The theme.** A lush pastoral. The theme is sung by woodwinds over harp and divided strings, and
it always resolves: B♭ → E♭ again and again, without tension, a melody that has nothing left to
want. Near the end, the theme is played once without its last note: B♭ B♭, B♭ B♭, and no E♭. A
question hangs in the air.

**Hand-over.** The unanswered B♭ is taken up by the Galactic age's strings, which answer it
with the whole of history.

**Interlude.** Harp and flute, the theme slowly and always resolved; once in a long while it
stops on the B♭ and waits.

*Story:* "Nothing costs anything any more. Food, metal, energy: there is enough of everything
for everyone. What is left to want? Time still passes, buildings still take time to raise, and
rivals still have ambitions. Plenty has not ended history. It has only changed what history is
about."

## 13. Galactic Empires — the finale, and ascension

**Forces.** *Social:* humanity everywhere and divided again, into rival empires spanning stars.
*Economic:* empire-scale wonders, the wealth of whole systems. *Political:* empire against
empire: the cycle of the Classical age repeated across the galaxy. At the end, one way out:
**ascension**, leaving everything behind.

**Outcome.** Grandeur and its limit: humanity has done everything it can do with matter, and
some choose to go beyond.

**Perception.** *Bright:* glory, the summit of history, humanity's greatest works. *Dark:* the
fall of empires, Rome again on a galactic scale. Over both stands ascension: the one exit from
the cycle. The music sums up everything and then lets it go.

**Art form.** The grand symphonic finale: everything that came before, in one movement.

**The theme.** The strings answer Abundance's unfinished question, and the whole orchestra joins
them in a vast tutti of the theme. Then history passes in review in the orchestra, each age a few bars in its own colours:
the Ancient drone and reed pipe, the Classical lyre (harp), the Medieval bell, the Discovery consort,
the Enlightenment Allegro, the Industrial train, the Modern march, the Freedom hook, the
Automation canon, the Fusion glow, the Spacefaring choir, the Starfaring march. All of it is
gathered into one last tutti of the theme, the grandest in the game.

**Ascension (coda).** Everything falls away: the orchestra thins and rises, and the theme is
left alone, unaccompanied, as it began: a single whistled line, B♭ B♭, B♭ B♭ E♭ E♭. The
ascended player begins again in the first age, and so does the music: the last sound is the
Ancient age's distant reed-pipe call.

**Interlude.** Low strings and horns in a slow, majestic version of the theme, with fragments
of the earlier ages' colours drifting through, like a memory.

*Story:* "Humanity rules the stars, and humanity is divided again. Empires span whole systems
and raise wonders that can be seen from other worlds. Rome rose and fell; so will they. But
for those who have learned everything there is to learn, there is another way: ascend, leave
it all behind, and begin again."

## The main title

The heroic statement of the theme without an age is the main title. The accepted sketch has a
solo trumpet, then brass over galloping strings, a ♭VI–♭VII–I lift and a unison tutti. The
final score keeps its shape (the bare statement, the lift, the unison) but is re-scored
symphonically, not as a fanfare: the strings carry the gallop and the melody, and the trumpet is
one voice in the orchestra. It plays in the launcher, which can also play each age's main theme
in turn.
